The method

How we find the money leaking
out of your admin.

Five phases, in order. Every phase ends with the exact steps to do it yourself: the same method we run for clients. Use the page either way.

Phase 1 · Measure

Find out where the week actually goes.

Not from memory. From a log. For five working days, note the interruptions: the chase, the re-type, the "where's my job up to" call. Ten seconds an entry, or a 90-second catch-up in the evening. The tracker is right here:

Or open it full-page: the Time Leak Tracker. No account, no email: data stays in your browser.

Do it yourself

  • Pick a normal week: no holidays, no deadline week
  • Log every interruption for 5 working days (estimate mode is fine, label it)
  • Read the day-3 report, then the day-5 one
  • Ask: which of these would I pay someone £15/hour to stop doing?

Cost: £0 · Time: ~10 min total

Phase 2 · Rank

Decide what's worth automating first.

Three questions rank any task: how much time does it eat, what happens if AI gets it wrong, and how rule-based is it. Score them here. The maths is visible, nothing is hidden:

Task Hrs/wk If AI gets it wrong Rule-based?

Scoring: hours/week × risk factor (low 1.0 · medium 0.6 · high 0.3) × rule-based factor (fully 1.0 · partly 0.6 · not 0.2). High score = automate first. A "not rule-based" task with real risk scores near zero on purpose. Judgment calls stay human. Starter rules, shown so you can argue with them.

Do it yourself

  • List every recurring admin task from your week's log
  • Score each: hours, blast radius if wrong, how rule-based it is
  • Anything judgment-based (pricing, who-gets-what): leave it alone

Cost: £0 · Time: 20 min

Phase 3 · Build small

One automation, live in 1–2 weeks.

Don't build a "system". Build the one thing that scored highest, measure it against last month, then decide. Small proof first, bigger gains next. Never asked to bet on a promise.

Do it yourself

  • Pick the top-ranked task. Nothing else
  • Write down today's baseline: how many, how long, who does it
  • Build with draft-only output first: nothing sends for two weeks
  • Measure after 2 weeks against the baseline; keep, fix, or kill

Cost: your evenings · Time: 1–2 weeks

Phase 4 · Keep a human in the loop

Approval first. Always, at the start.

Every send starts as a draft that waits for a person. Once you trust a routine category, you switch it to auto, per category, your choice. Money, compliance and anything sensitive stay approval-only permanently. See the approve-flow working:

The morning-brief demo: scan, draft, approve or edit, all in the browser.

Do it yourself

  • Draft mode on everything for at least two weeks
  • Only flip a category to auto after a spotless fortnight
  • Keep money, compliance and legal approval-only forever

Cost: £0 · Time: discipline

Phase 5 · Monitor & compound

Measure monthly. Add the next one.

Automations drift: inboxes change, a supplier renames a form, a team member invents a new workaround. A monthly look at the numbers catches it, and the next-ranked task from Phase 2 becomes the next build. This is the part that compounds.

Do it yourself

  • Re-run the tracker one day a month and compare
  • Fix what drifted before it quietly rots
  • Pick the next task off the Phase-2 list when capacity allows

Cost: £0 · Time: ~1 hr/month

Our own five mistakes. Made here, so you skip them.

The same honesty we'd want from anyone we paid.

  • Building for the edge case first. The weird 5% of cases is where builds go to die. Ship the boring 95%, keep a human for the rest.
  • Automating a judgment call. The one time AI priced the job, we paid for the lesson. If it needs judgment, it needs a human. Permanently.
  • Shipping without a review step. An automation with no approval gate is a machine for making mistakes faster. Draft mode isn't caution, it's the design.
  • Scoping from a chat instead of data. "Walk me through your week" from memory is the least reliable data there is. That's why the tracker exists.
  • Treating it as a project with an end date. Inboxes change, tools update, people improvise. Whatever isn't maintained drifts: the maintenance is the product.

What this method won't give you.

  • No £10/month magic. Real automations need real setup and a real person keeping them honest. Anyone promising otherwise is selling you the trial.
  • Nothing automated that needs judgment. Pricing, hiring, which client gets the good slot: that stays yours. We mark it "leave it alone" in writing.
  • No fake precision. Estimates are labelled as estimates, the maths behind every number is visible, and there's no "readiness score out of 100" because nobody can defend one.
  • No replacing your team. The point is the repetitive work off their plate, not fewer people. If a pitch implies otherwise, walk away.
