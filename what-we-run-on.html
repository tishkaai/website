What we run our own business on

For UK owners deciding who to believe from an AI company with no clients yet: the three things our own business runs on, and the honest cost of running them.

Last updated 12 September 2026

It's 7am on a Saturday and the first thing on the screen is a brief nobody wrote by hand: what happened overnight, what needs a decision today. Every piece of work this business does lands in a ledger, one line at a time, as it happens. The whole thing lives in plain files we own. That is the loop this post is about, because it is the same design we build for clients.

The morning brief. Every morning, a brief generates itself: what happened overnight, what needs a decision today. It's read with coffee, before the first message of the day arrives. One honest wrinkle: the scheduler that fires it has missed mornings, so some days it is generated by hand instead. The design survives that, because the rules are written down.

The ledger. Every unit of work is logged as it happens: what was built, what it was for, when. When someone asks what happened this week, the answer is a read, not a memory test. A week of rows takes five minutes to review.

Plain files. The whole business brain is files we own: portable, no lock-in. If every tool shut down tomorrow, the knowledge would still be ours. We build client brains the same way: files you keep.

And the rule through all of it: nothing sends unchecked. Agent work goes propose, then approve. A person taps. Today that person is Pete; the design assumes a team, so the tap transfers as it grows. Financial, compliance and legal never leave approval-only, on ourselves included.

The maths (a sketch, not a client figure)

What running the system on ourselves costs and saves, directionally:

  • The habit costs: 2 minutes to read the brief each morning, plus under a minute to log each piece of work: 5 minutes a day, 25 minutes a week at £30 an hour = £12.50 a week, about £575 a year.
  • The habit replaces: the manual morning sweep, opening each inbox and system in turn: about 15 minutes a day = 75 minutes a week = £37.50 a week, about £1,725 a year.
  • Plus the ledger: "what happened this week?" becomes a five-minute read instead of an hour of reconstruction.

Net sketch: £12.50 a week of habit replacing £37.50 a week of checks. Swap in your own numbers. (A sketch, not a client figure.)

Score it: the three checks

Score the loop, assembling the day's status by hand, on the three checks:

  • Hours. The manual version, checking every system in turn, is about 15 minutes a day (3).
  • Repeat. The same places every day; a new starter could follow it from one page (5).
  • Risk-if-wrong. A missed item costs a day or two, and the underlying systems still hold the truth (3).

Two checks at 4 or more, so the verdict is start narrow: brief the inbox alone for a week, then add sources one at a time. That is how we built ours.

Try one thing this week (free)

Write tomorrow's brief yourself. Tonight, before you close the laptop, write two lines: what happened today, and what needs a decision tomorrow. Tomorrow, do your normal checks and compare. Anything your note missed is the first line of the brief you should hand over. Judge it on Friday: three mornings of this tells you whether a two-minute brief would have caught your week.

Where a person stays

Approval-first is not a policy page on our site. It is how the system runs, on ourselves first. Software assembles the brief, drafts the ledger rows, prepares the work. A person reads, edits and approves. Nothing sends, deletes or spends unchecked, and financial, compliance and legal never leave approval-only. If the design is good enough to sell, it is already carrying our own week.

FAQ

You're pre-launch. Why should anyone copy how you run things?

Because there is no client list to point at, the design has to stand on its own: what we run on, what it costs us, what failed. The £499 diagnostic carries 5 hours found or full refund, so the risk of finding out sits with us.

What does the morning brief actually contain?

What happened overnight, what needs a decision today, and anything that has slipped past a date: an invoice past its terms, a client gone quiet, a deadline two days out. Two minutes to read, before the first message of the day.

Isn't a ledger just admin for the sake of admin?

It is why "what happened this week?" takes five minutes to answer instead of an hour. Written as work happens, it becomes the company's diary. Asked in November, it still remembers September.

Why plain files instead of a proper platform?

Because whoever does the work should own the record. Plain files mean no lock-in, and the knowledge survives even if the tools do not. Client brains get built the same way: files you keep.

Does the AI ever act on its own?

No. Propose, then approve, on everything. Financial, compliance and legal stay approval-only, always, on us same as on a client.
