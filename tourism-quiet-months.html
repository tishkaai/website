What a West Wales tourism business can automate in the quiet months

For tourism businesses from Cardigan to Tenby: the season's end is the one window when there's time to fix the admin. What's worth setting up before spring.

Last updated 14 September 2026

October in a West Wales visitor town has a different shape to August. The changeover days stop. The phone slows. The same owner who spent July answering questions at midnight suddenly has an hour with no one waiting in it. Most businesses spend that hour on maintenance and invoices, which is fair, and then spring arrives and July runs exactly like last July.

The quiet months are the only time of year a tourism business can build anything. Next season's calm is made now, in the gaps.

The maths (a sketch, not a client figure)

Take one season's repeating admin that could have been prepared in advance: guest answers, review replies, booking enquiries, supplier reorders.

  • × 30 minutes a day across the season = 3.5 hours a week
  • × 20 season weeks = 70 hours a year
  • × £25 an hour = £1,750 a season

Preparing the machinery in the quiet months doesn't add hours in October. It removes them in August, when an hour is the most expensive thing the business owns.

Score it: the three checks

  • Hours. Any job that took real minutes every week of the season: the same guest questions, the same review replies, the same reorder emails.
  • Repeat. The season is the same shape every year. The questions barely change from one summer to the next, which makes them writable.
  • Risk-if-wrong. A stale price in an auto-reply is the risk to design around. That's why every draft waits for a person, and why the facts page is updated before the tool is.

Try one thing this week (free)

Write the facts page for next season before you forget this season's arguments: check-in times, parking, the Wi-Fi, the dog policy, what's open in the village. Then save your five most-sent replies of the summer underneath it. That document costs one quiet afternoon and it is the raw material for everything: the automated drafts, the new website copy, the training page for next year's cover.

Where a person stays

Nothing goes out unchecked. In season, the drafts wait for approval before a guest sees them, and in the quiet months the same rule holds for review replies and booking enquiries. The owner approves, the software remembers. When next season's rush comes, the approving takes a minute and the composing has already been done.

FAQ

Why set this up in the quiet months rather than mid-season?

Mid-season, every hour spent on tools is an hour taken from guests. In the quiet months the same work costs nothing, and it can be watched for a few weeks before the season stress-tests it.

We close over winter. Is there anything to automate?

Yes, and it's the easy stretch: enquiries for next year still arrive, reviews still land, suppliers still need reordering for spring. The low season generates the routine messages too, just slower.

What's worth preparing first?

The facts page and the season's top replies. Everything else builds on them. A tool with no facts page just makes confident nonsense faster, so the writing comes before any wiring.

What if it isn't ready by spring?

Then it waits, and nothing is lost: the facts page and the chase rhythms still work by hand. Nothing here should go live mid-season untested. Start narrow in spring, one kind of message, watched for a fortnight.
