The cold snap that fills a heating engineer's phone by nine

For heating engineers and plumbers across Ceredigion and west Wales: the first proper frost turns the phone into a queue, and the queue decides your day.

Last updated 3 November 2026

The first cold morning in Cardigan is easy to spot. The phone starts before the van is loaded. No heat, no hot water, a boiler showing a fault code, a radiator that never came on, and the same question underneath all of it: when can you get here?

By nine the list is long. Some calls are quick. Some are voicemails you will listen to twice. Some are texts with a photo of a boiler and no address. Every one of them needs an answer, and the answer is rarely the job itself. It is a slot, a question, or a polite no.

That is the loop: the cold-morning call back. It is not the repair. It is the hour or two before the repair, when the day gets decided by whoever is holding the phone.

The maths (a sketch, not a client figure)

Take a cold morning with twelve calls before nine. Some are two minutes, some are eight. A fair average for the whole dance, including the text back with the address and the note in the diary, is about seven minutes a call.

  • 12 calls at 7 minutes is 84 minutes
  • 20 calls, which happens after a hard frost, is 2 hours 20
  • at £45 an hour for the time the van is not earning, 84 minutes is about £63
  • on the cold mornings, the lost time is closer to £105

Those are assumptions, not measurements from a real business. The point is the shape. The calls are not the work. They are the gate in front of the work, and the gate is narrow on the days you can least afford it.

Score it: the three checks

  • Hours. An hour on a normal cold day, two or more after a hard frost, and it repeats through every cold week from November to March. That clears the bar for a done-for-you build.
  • Repeat. The questions repeat. Is it the boiler or the thermostat? Is there a fault code? Is the pressure low? Where is the property? Can you come today? The answers repeat too, which is exactly what makes a written list work.
  • Risk-if-wrong. A missed call is a lost job, and a wrong answer about a gas appliance is not a small mistake. The technical advice has to come from the qualified engineer. The slot, the address and the reminder can be handled by the office. That split is the design.

Try one thing this week (free)

Write the five questions your callers always answer, in the order you ask them. Then add the three things you need before you can give a slot: the postcode, the boiler make and model, and whether the caller is at the property.

Put that on one sheet by the phone. Whoever answers reads from the sheet, writes the answers down, and gives a call-back window rather than a promise. You will still be busy. You will just stop asking the same five questions from memory while the van sits idle.

Where a person stays

Software can take the call details, spot the repeat questions, prepare the text with the slot and put the job in the diary. It cannot decide whether a fault is safe, and it should not try.

The engineer approves every slot and every piece of technical advice. Anything about gas, electrical work, or a safety concern goes straight to the qualified person, with no draft in between. The customer gets a clear time and a named person. Nothing is sent as a promise the engineer has not agreed to.

That is the split we build to: the machine handles the queue, the person handles the judgement.

FAQ

Do we need new software to start?

No. The first version is a written call sheet and a shared diary. If that is already enough, you do not need us. If the queue is still eating the morning, the sheet is the spec for the build.

What about a real emergency, like a smell of gas?

That never waits in a queue. It goes to a person immediately and follows your gas safety procedure. No automation sits in front of an emergency.

Does this work for a one-van business?

Yes. The smaller the team, the more the morning matters, because there is nobody else to answer the phone while you are under a boiler.

Will it answer the phone for us?

It can take the details and send the slot, but we would not let it give technical advice. Your engineer stays the person who decides what is wrong and what to do.
