The 6am shift-cover phone call in Ceredigion

For care, cleaning and hospitality firms in Ceredigion: how one sick call turns into a morning of ringing round, and what a written cover order changes.

Last updated 25 September 2026

It is ten past six in Cardigan. A text arrives. The person who opens up on a Tuesday is ill. The owner is on the phone before the kettle has boiled. Two rings to Lampeter, no answer. A message to Aberaeron. A call to Aberystwyth, where someone can cover the first half of the shift but not the second. So the round starts again.

That is the loop: shift cover and rota changes, worked out of one person's memory at the worst hour of the day. In a team of six or eight, the same names get rung in roughly the same order every time. The order lives in someone's head. On a tired morning the ringing round takes longer and the gaps get missed.

By eight the shift is covered. The first proper job of the day has not started. Two mornings like that in a week and the week is short before it begins.

The maths (a sketch, not a client figure)

These are assumptions, not measured figures. Change them to match your own week.

Assume one absence costs about an hour of ringing round, and the owner's time is worth £30 an hour.

  • × 1 hour per absence = the time cost
  • × 2 absences a week = 2 hours
  • × £30 an hour = £60 a week
  • × 46 working weeks = £2,760 a year

The same morning with a draft step looks different. The names and what each person can cover sit on one page. The software lines up the next name and writes the message. A person reads it and decides whether it goes.

Score it: the three checks

  • Hours. One absence eats about an hour, and two in a week eat two, before the rota is even rewritten. Most small teams clear that bar on the first absent Monday.
  • Repeat. The cover order is a short list: who to ring first, who can open and who cannot do weekends. If it fits on one page, a new starter can run it.
  • Risk-if-wrong. Ringing the wrong person wastes ten minutes. Moving someone into a shift they are not cleared for is a different size of mistake. That is what decides who checks it.

Try one thing this week (free)

Write your cover order on one page. Put the names in the order you ring them, and beside each name what they can cover and when they cannot. Keep it by the phone. By Friday you will know whether it saved you a call.

Where a person stays

The software holds the list, remembers who was asked last and writes the message. A named person decides who moves, checks that the move is safe and legal for that shift, and approves the wording before anything is sent. Financial, compliance and legal work stays approval-only, always. Cover for a regulated shift is the same: the software can propose the move, and a person signs it off.

FAQ

What if nobody on the list can cover?

The draft stops and comes back to the owner as an open gap, with the names already tried and the times still uncovered. It does not keep messaging people.

Does the software move people between shifts on its own?

No. It writes the message and shows the change. A named person approves the move before it happens.

Can it handle a rota that changes through the day?

Yes, if the changes follow a pattern you can write down. That written page is what makes it work.
